A field that nothing reads and nothing writes is a dangling object, not a lying instrument, and must be described as such. A constant whose scale shipped telemetry contradicts, such as HDEFMaxIntegrity 12 against a shipped range of 12 to 99, is a balance change: report it with the evidence and do not apply it. The backend folder is named ReactorBackend; it was ReferenceContent until 2026-09-26. HDEF integrity is a 0-100 scale, stated by the user on 2026-09-26 and corroborated by the shipped telemetry, so HDEFMaxIntegrity 100 supersedes 12 and the earlier instruction to report that constant rather than apply it is discharged. A constant that encodes a maximum must not be shadowed by a literal derived from its old value: the HDEF lamp step is computed from the constant rather than written beside it, because the literal 4 was a quarter of 12 and would have lit three lamps on a nearly empty generator. A published value that sits one short of its ceiling because recovery clamps at the maximum and decay is subtracted in the same step is a rounding display, not a scale error, and is recorded rather than corrected. A documented failure path that has never been observed being set is not evidence that it is unreachable: hdef.fault is named by the in-game manual and was reached for the first time only when a replay ran with the outtake fans off and the three C-Pumps on, because the generator's 100 second heat clock races the PEA stress clock and loses by about nine seconds while the pumps are idle. A failure the manual assigns to the operator as a duty, such as disabling the outtake fans in State 1 to avoid stallout, is authored tension and not a defect, and the control that reverses the sign of the pressure derivative is the one to name when explaining it. A command chain gated by a phase machine must be spaced in time rather than issued in one burst: three true results followed by a false one is the signature of a phase gate rather than of a rejected control, and a trace that never reaches the expected phase has already said so. A motion constant must be measured from the rig it is applied to, and one lever to another: a throw copied across a facility fits no lever, and the way to catch it is to ask whether the pose being drawn exists anywhere in the authored geometry. Where a fixed anchor and a moving part are both authored, the sign of the throw falls out of which one the anchor is attached to, so the direction is a property of the art and must be read per rig rather than derived from the engine. A measurement taken only on levers that happen to rest at their home position is a measurement of the sample and not of the rig. A document that describes a folder as absent while the folder is being read out of is a failure of a different kind from drift, and correcting it belongs to the same change rather than to a later tidy-up.

A click that is refused and a click that never happened are indistinguishable from the outside, so the only honest way to test the input chain is to count the event at its source: a listener on the detector itself, reporting a number, is what turns "the lamp changed" into "the click arrived". When a client's own copy of a value disagrees with the server's, the client's copy is not evidence of anything -- a character teleported by the client reads at the new position to the client and at the old one to the server, and it is the server's number that every gate is measured against, which is why a position the player must be at is established server-side and allowed to replicate rather than set locally and hoped for. Two coordinate spaces that look like one are worse than two that obviously differ: a raycast proving a part is under a pixel is not proof that the input system agrees, because the input system may be measuring the same pixel in a space of its own, and the thing that matters is which space the consumer reads. An engine that documents a seam for tests should be tested through that seam, and a private state driven through it is a truer test than the live one, because the live one carries whatever the last player did. A probe must repeat the addresses it checks rather than ask the module where its own parts are, since a probe that asks cannot detect the module having been given the wrong address. A result that was never read is not a result, and a claim of coverage that rests on an unread output is the failure this project has already paid for once.

A threshold table has a unit and the unit decides everything: a list of constants measures nothing until the question of which of two published quantities it is compared against has been answered, and where the two differ by a factor the answer is not visible in the constants at all -- it is visible in the gaps the data leaves, so measure the gaps between adjacent states and ask which candidate falls inside them rather than which candidate looks like a round number. A log that never enters the regime a question is about cannot answer that question however many records it holds: 477 ticks settled two boundaries completely and are structurally silent on the third, because the quantity that would have decided it never reached the value the question is about, and a search of that source for the answer is a search that cannot succeed rather than one that has not yet succeeded. An event log is evidence of naming and never evidence of completeness: a record that captures 24 clicks and not the startup click of the reactor it shows being started cannot be read as an inventory, and an absent entry is not an absent event, which is the trap of concluding from silence. A quantity missing from the source that would have measured it is not missing data, it is evidence that the shipped game did not model it, so a constant no upstream source can validate should be labelled as an invention of this rewrite rather than hunted for, and the hunt should stop when the source's whole schema has been enumerated rather than when patience runs out. Where the original's model of a failure can be read, prefer reporting it over the invented scalar: a fault that is an event with a visible consequence and a recovery that restores less than was lost is a different mechanic from a value drifting toward a threshold, and the difference is worth recording even when the rewrite keeps its own. Two fields that agree to within their own rounding are one quantity seen twice, and the test is the sign and the bound of the residual: a difference that is never negative and never exceeds the smaller unit's step is a floor and a round of a single float, not two simulations that happen to correlate, and a field sitting at a constant floor while another climbs is clamped and not measuring.

An instrument that shares an initialization step with the system it measures must perform that step itself: the same module is only the same module after something has initialized it, and a probe that reads a table nobody filled reports the authored default as a measurement, which yields a full page of plausible failures and no error at all. A failure that is uniform across every case is a property of the instrument and not of the subject: thirteen lamps each reading one constant colour, and every position comparison reading exactly zero, are two symptoms of one empty table -- and the tell is the case that passes rather than the case that fails, because a lamp cannot read a palette entry by accident unless nothing painted it, so when a probe passes a case whose expected colour is the part's own authored colour, read that as the instrument and not as the subject. A routine that measures from live state is not idempotent unless it is written to be: reading a sign off a part's current position returns a different answer once anything has moved the part, so what has already been measured must not be measured again while the part is still there, and a second call is safe only when it does nothing. A counter an operator watches for loss must be comparable between calls to the same function, because an increment inside a conditional branch reads differently on the second call and turns the one stable number into a moving one. Two documents that are updated together and are not byte-mirrors will drift, and the drift is visible only by counting sections, so count them.


A hash taken before the last line cannot be compared against a hash of the whole file, and the resulting mismatch is indistinguishable from corruption: the receipt was written with the hash as it stood before the receipt itself was appended, so recomputing over the file could never match, and the first verifier run reported a lost byte count against a file whose EVT ids were consecutive and whose line and event counts agreed with the producer exactly. The lesson is not to be careful about off-by-one but to have the artefact state its own coverage -- the receipt now carries over=<bytes> beside hash=<value> -- and to assert the boundary rather than assume it, because an integrity check whose two sides measure different things is the same tautology this project has already been burned by, wearing the opposite costume. A counter that disagrees with the receiver's log cannot be used to check anything: the segment counter was incremented only inside the disk path, so a run that delivered every byte over HTTP reported segments=0 in its own receipt while the receiver logged seventy-four of them, and the fix is to count on both paths rather than to trust the one that was instrumented. A local declared below its use becomes a global reading nil, and the two halves of the file look identical while using different variables -- four names in this script were exactly that, so the check belongs in a script and not in a reading of the diff, and a static sweep for identifiers in value position that are never declared local caught them in one pass. Ordering matters inside the checker too: strip strings before comments, because the reverse eats the closing quote of any string containing a double dash and silently swallows the code after it, which is how the first version of that sweep reported a clean file by not seeing most of it. A probe that reads its own counters in the same call that started the work reads zero, because a spawned coroutine has not been given a turn yet -- which is the same error as reading a module table in a different VM, in a new costume: the state is live and correct and simply not where the question was asked, so a status read is its own call and never a line after the line that started the thing being measured. A startup path with no pcall is worse than one that fails, because a recorder that dies between loaded and recording looks exactly like a recorder that is working, and there is no console left to read afterwards.
 Six things learned from the readout-sweep flood, in the order they were paid for. A statistic about a key belongs to the key and not to whatever object currently references it: the chatter guard counters lived on the readout record, the monitors rebuild their screen labels several times a second as their glitch animation, and every rebuild constructed fresh records starting at polls=0 and dead=false, so the guard clock was wiped from underneath it and it never reached a verdict -- measured at 424 polls with the file growing at 543 lines a second and zero NOISY events, which is a guard working perfectly on a counter that no longer exists. A counter incremented on one of two paths reports zero, and this is the third occurrence in this project: polls was incremented inside the numeric branch only, so every animated label, which is every label whose text is not a number, left polls at 0 forever and could never equal the threshold the guard compared it against -- the same shape as the segments counter that was incremented only inside diskSegment, a counter that has a comment in this very file warning about it, which is the point: a warning in a comment does not prevent the next instance of the bug it warns about, only a check does. A guard judged after a long grace period pays the full cost before it can act, so the grace period is part of the defect rather than a safety margin on it: the old guard waited 240 polls, a full minute, and that minute was 39975 TEXT events and 8 MB, with one GlitchFrame label alone accounting for 1.1 MB. A threshold is worth deriving rather than choosing, and both of the new ones come from one fact -- the game ticks at 1 Hz while the recorder polls at 4 Hz, so a value that is really a value can move at most once per game tick, which puts the rate arm four-per-poll and the persistence arm fifty per cent where they are with headroom instead of by taste. Retrying a failed send without yielding turns a symptom into its own cause: flush was called synchronously from emit, so each retry fired in the same instant the previous one failed, and a sink that had merely fallen behind for a moment never got the gap it needed to catch up -- the remedy is a backoff keyed on the consecutive-failure counter, which is exactly the right exponent because that counter is already reset on every success. And the test rig is part of the experiment: two receive.py processes were listening on 127.0.0.1:8765 simultaneously, which allow_reuse_address permits on Windows, and a duplicated listener that never accepts looks precisely like a recorder that cannot deliver, which is how 2123 consecutive post failures were first read as a transport defect while the transport answered 20 of 20 posts in 0.35 s the moment the storm stopped. Two smaller rules fall out of the same run: report two counts where two things are counted, because found=1115 against distinct=923 settles in one line a question that several rounds of inference had not, and compare two runs at equal poll counts rather than equal wall time, because the game state differs between them and the difference will otherwise be attributed to the change.

A friend's client-side modification is admissible evidence for EXISTENCE and never for values, and the distinction is the difference between using it and copying it. Addition/Shift4.luau proves that a click fired from the client reaches the server in this game, because the consoles it drives are server-side systems that a client-only change could not move in any way a player would see -- that is a claim about the game and it is now settled. It does not carry a fireclickdetector implementation, only a warning stub, so it cannot settle what that function does, how it is spelled at the executor, or whether the executor in use has one, and the driver therefore probes for the global instead of assuming it. Two consequences follow for any artefact inherited from someone else: its paths are leads to confirm in the live place rather than constants to hard-code, which is why the driver's switch lists are built by searching a subtree for a name pattern; and its technique, the SelectRandom that counts PW<i>ClickPart children instead of naming one, is the part worth taking, while its coordinates and its specific control names are the part that rots.

A proof window that accepts an absent sensor has proved nothing, and this is the third face of the same defect this project has now paid for: isRunning is false both when the core is off and when the temperature readout is missing, so an unguarded window reads a dead sensor as a successful press -- the value is live and correct where it is computed and simply cannot answer the question being asked at the place the question is asked, which is the shape shared by reading a module table in the wrong VM and by reading a counter in the call that started the work. The remedy is to guard the arm that infers from a signal rather than the arm that compares two readings, because a comparison against nil is already false while an inference from nil is silently a verdict. Beside it: a state machine that only a test can find the end of is worth writing the test for, because the failure it hides is the one that costs the whole run -- the cold-start path parked in a state waiting for an edge that the path it had taken never produces, and no amount of reading the states finds it, because every state is individually correct and only the composition is wrong. So a harness for a shipped state machine must extract the function by TEXT out of the file that ships rather than copy it, since a copy passes against the copy; it must assert that the extraction is complete rather than that it compiles, since a truncated body that closes itself parses perfectly; and it must include one scenario per way the machine can END, because the endings are the only places a machine is observably wrong. And an unattended experiment must be built to end in a NAME rather than in silence: every press here is followed by a window whose failure names the candidate that did nothing and moves to the next, so the worst outcome of a run nobody watched is a report of which control is dead, and the one outcome that is forbidden is a recorder that pressed nothing, recorded nothing, and looks exactly like a healthy idle plant.

The absence of a key in a telemetry log and the absence of the quantity in the game are different claims, and this project has now made each of them once. PUMP_FAULT_INTEGRITY = 25 was recorded as an orphan invention on the strength of a real measurement -- no stability key appears in any leaf of all 477 shipped records -- and the measurement was sound while the conclusion was not, because TRGWeb declares a per-pump Stability and the console that displays it has been standing in Workspace the whole time. The rule this yields is to state which of the two things a negative proved: a log is a record of what some earlier author chose to write down, so its silence bounds the log and nothing else, whereas a sweep of the live place bounds the place. Both are worth doing and neither substitutes for the other, and a conclusion that travels from one to the other has to be labelled as the inference it is. A corollary about instruments: code that reads a value and never writes it is evidence about the value's EXISTENCE and about nothing else, so TRGWeb's coolant_pump1_Stability at 100 establishes that the quantity is real and that 100 is full, and establishes nothing whatever about how stability moves, because nothing in that file moves it. And unreferenced finished art is a specific and easily missed kind of finding: the NET STABILITY gauge is not a stub or a placeholder, it is a completed screen with a title, a labelled bar and a fill, and the only reason it looked absent was that no script named it -- so a search for a feature has to include a search for the geometry that would show it, because the parts are findable even when the code is not. Finally, a replacement anchored on a line NUMBER is anchored on nothing, since every earlier edit moves it: the splice that landed nine lines off and rewrote a different section was caught only because the script asserted the text it was replacing, and the order that avoids the class entirely is to splice from the bottom of the file upward.

A proof window must record the state it FOUND, not only the state it sees: an accept condition that tests a predicate which was already true when the window opened credits the action with a consequence it did not cause, and the more reliable the predicate, the more convincing the false accept. The driver pressed the shutdown switch on a core that had crossed below the running threshold eight seconds earlier and reported `shut accepted after 0.2s` -- 0.2 s is not a fast shutdown, it is the first polling interval at which a pre-existing condition is observed. The fix is not a longer window or a stricter threshold but a second fact: the state at the instant of the press, so that not-running-now can be told apart from not-running-now-AND-running-then. A measurement with a temporal direction needs both of its endpoints, and this is the fourth face of the same defect this project keeps paying for -- after a readout sweep that accepted an absent sensor, a telemetry log read as the absence of a quantity, and a length check that called two different payloads equal.

An arm of a condition that has never fired is a claim and not a safety net, and which one it is gets settled by measurement rather than by reading the code. isRunning was documented, for as long as the recorder has existed, as output above zero OR temperature above the first threshold; m.out appears in none of the four captures, so only the temperature arm has ever run. The harmless case is a dead arm nothing depends on. The one that matters is a dead arm that would have been WRONG if it were alive, and this was that: wiring the output up would have held isRunning permanently true in the 18:01 capture, where the output never fell below 60, and the DOWN phase would never have been detected at all -- a reactor with no flow to record, and no error anywhere to say so. The arm was therefore removed rather than repaired, and what justifies the removal is a measurement of the arm's effect, not a preference for shorter code.

A single-use action changes who is allowed to decide. The driver's whole purpose was to remove a human from the timing, and once the user reported that the core can be switched only ONCE, the driver's autonomy at the START became a liability rather than the feature: a recorder that spends an irreversible action the moment it is injected spends it before anyone has read what it found. The resolution separates the two halves -- a human presses DRIVER, and everything after that press, where the timing actually matters, stays unattended. Automation should absorb the part of a task a person cannot do and hand back the part only a person should decide; taking both is how an autonomous tool spends a resource its operator was saving.

WHEN A GUARD REJECTS SOMETHING YOU NEED, CHANGE THE GRANULARITY OF THE REPORT, NOT THE GUARD. The readout sweep drops any key that moves more than a few times inside one poll, and that arm is what keeps the file from carrying every scrolling label in the facility -- but it was dropping the subspace forecast, the one panel an operator would actually act on, as `EVT1506 ANIM ...ScrollingFrame.TimeSliceTemplate.TimeLabel moved 5 times inside one poll; dropped as an animation`. The cheap fix is to raise the threshold; that would have cost the arm its whole purpose to recover one panel. The resolution keeps the arm and reshapes what is reported: the panel goes out as a single joined snapshot with one key, so it can change at most once per poll and the arm has nothing left to reject. The flood is still stopped everywhere else and the panel that was missing is present. A guard that fires on the wrong thing is usually saying the thing being reported is the wrong SHAPE, and the answer is to reshape the report -- loosening the guard instead trades the failure you can see for the one you cannot.

`X and Y or Z` CANNOT RETURN A NIL Y, AND THE CASE IT COULD NOT EXPRESS WAS THE ONE IT WAS WRITTEN FOR. The attribution pass guarded its only interesting branch -- a client that has never seen a click, which is the 18:01 case the whole section exists for -- with `local since = (clickPoll == nil) and nil or (sample - clickPoll)`. Lua yields the middle operand only when it is truthy, so with nil in the middle the expression falls through to `sample - nil` and throws. It shipped like that. The idiom is correct in six other places in the same file, which is precisely why reading it did not catch it: a form that works in six uses out of seven does not read as a mistake, it reads as house style. What caught it was a harness that executes the shipped bytes, on the first call, before any of it reached the one attempt available -- and the caller is not pcall-wrapped, so in the game the outcome would have been a dead poll, a dead recording, and no error anyone would ever see. The lesson is not to avoid the idiom. It is that an idiom's general correctness is not evidence about any particular use of it, and that a branch whose distinguishing feature is that its value is ABSENT cannot be written in a form that has no way to produce absence.

THE FIRST RUN OF A NEW TEST IS ALLOWED TO FAIL ON THE TEST, AND THE FIX IS NEVER TO LOOSEN THE ASSERTION. Four of nineteen panel assertions failed on the first run and all four were defects in the test. One compared two snapshots that held the same three labels in a different enumeration order and read their equality as a bug -- the equality was the property under test. Three ran both truncation caps at once, so with 250 short rows the character cap bit at row 235 and the assertion written for the row cap was reading the character cap's marker; the `%%` in those two needles was a Lua pattern escape for a literal percent sign, so they could not have matched anything. With four red assertions against code that had already been read through, the temptation is to relax the assertions until they go green. Every one was instead fixed by making the scenario match what the assertion claimed to be testing, because an assertion bent to fit the code stops being evidence -- and a test that cannot fail is worse than no test, since it is still counted as coverage. Both harnesses now also fail the PROCESS and not only the page, for the same reason: a harness whose only failure signal is a word in its output passes in every script that runs it.

A NUMBER THE PROGRAM WRITES ABOUT ITSELF IS A CLAIM, AND IT HAS TO COME FROM THE SAME PLACE THE BEHAVIOUR DOES. The driver advanced every dwell by Config.SampleInterval, and the loop it lived in ran at 3.38 Hz against that configured 0.25 s -- so `DriveHoldSeconds = 60` announced in the log at t=2.84 was followed by its press at t=73.43, and the 70.59 s in between is the size of the lie. Nothing failed. The reactor was switched off correctly, the recording is complete, the receipt is accurate about the plant. The only wrong thing was the recorder's account of its own timing, which is precisely the account a reader trusts instead of recomputing -- and it was caught not by any check but by a person noticing that two numbers in the same file disagreed about how long a duration was. The fix removes the constant from the driver entirely and passes in a step the loop measures, because a configured period is a claim about a loop and only the loop can report what the loop did. The general form: whenever a program states a fact about its own execution, that fact must be derived from the execution and not from the configuration that was supposed to describe it.

THE CLOCK A TEST CANNOT AFFORD TO WAIT ON IS STILL THE CLOCK THE CODE MUST READ -- SO PUT IT IN THE CALLER. Moving the measured step into driveStep directly would have been the smaller change and the wrong one: driveStep is executed by a harness against a simulated core, and a function that reads the wall clock internally can only be tested in real time, so a 300-second scenario would cost 300 seconds and the suite would stop being run. Handing the step in as a parameter costs one argument and buys three things at once -- the loop owns the clock and there is exactly one place that reads it, the function stays a pure step, and the defect becomes testable at all, because the harness can hand it a one-second step and assert that the dwell follows the step rather than the constant. That last one is the point: the timing error was invisible for a whole run and became a one-line assertion only after the dependency was inverted. When a piece of code is hard to test, the obstacle is usually a dependency pointing the wrong way rather than a missing test.

REMOVING AN ASSUMPTION FROM ONE PLACE EXPOSES THE ASSUMPTIONS THAT WERE RESTING ON IT, AND THOSE ARE YOURS TOO. The driver took its step from a constant; measuring it instead was right, and it was measured because the constant had made the one live run's dwells eighteen per cent long. Hours later, re-reading that change against the arming branch, the same edit turned out to have removed a load-bearing side effect nobody had declared: the constant also guaranteed that no single step could be large, which was the only thing keeping the debounce window wider than one poll. The arm window could therefore expire on a core that was reading as running, and the driver's response to that is to refuse and go inert -- on the one attempt that was available. Accuracy in one place had bought fragility in another, and the fragility was invisible because it lived in a property of the old value rather than in any line of code. The rule this leaves behind: when a constant becomes a measurement, enumerate what the constant was implicitly guaranteeing before shipping the measurement. A fixed value is rarely only a value; it is usually also a bound, and the bound is the part nobody wrote down.
