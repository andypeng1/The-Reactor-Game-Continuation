2026-09-26 lamp proof: the thirteen lamps carried as unproven since they were wired -- shutter, mute, atmo, vent1-3, the two gravatron lamps, sam1-2 and the three CBL pressure buttons -- are proven, 38 of 38, by the server-side probe in ServerScriptService.MCP_LampProbe. They were unproven because the probe was wrong rather than because the lamps were: a Server Script starts in an undefined order, this one lost a race with Runtime, and it ran Refresh against a VisualFeedback whose levers and lamps tables were still empty, so every poseLever and lightLamp returned without touching a part and the probe read each part's authored colour, constant across every case, which is exactly what a broken lamp looks like. Every startup comparison read delta 0.0000 for the same reason, five of them counted as passes because nothing had moved and the sixth, starting != cold, as a failure. The tell was the two colour cases that passed: atmo while running and cbl1 nominal both read Nominal, because AtmosphereVentButton.NeonPart and the CBL pressure button neons are authored 137,255,147 and Config.Visual.Nominal was measured off them, so a lamp cannot read a palette entry by accident unless nothing painted it. The probe now calls Initialize itself, prints any error Refresh throws instead of dying silently, and reports its own key count rather than using # on a string-keyed table. Proved family by family: shutter closed to Amber and open to Off; mute off to Off and on to Fault; atmo cold to Off, running to Nominal, and inside its own cooldown back to Off; vent1-3 unused to Off and spent to Fault; the CBL buttons Off while inactive and Nominal, Yellow at PW5, Purple on stress, Blue below stall pressure and Fault, with all four precedence pairs each beating the branch it must; gravSwitch Off at Idle and Ready at Charging and Armed; gravArmed Off except Yellow at Armed; and samLamp1-2 Off then Nominal together. The master start-up lever is proved the same way, 1.686 studs exactly TRAVEL.start, moved in Starting, Running and Stopping and at delta 0.0000 against Cold in Cold, Booting, Ready and Report. The real defect fell out of the same run: Initialize was not idempotent. Two Initialize calls in one playtest disagreed about the six fans, minus 0.658 first and plus 0.700 second, because smallThrow reads a SmallLever sign from the union's current position, and Runtime's own first Refresh had already driven the fans to their far stop since the engine boots with the fans running, so the second capture measured the rig standing on its other stop and inverted the travel of every fan for the rest of the session, leaving a lever that still moves on a click but moves the wrong way onto a pose the art does not contain, with nothing in the console reporting it. That also explains, after the fact, the flapping signs in the earlier boot log, which had been attributed to displaced levers in the place: an Edit-mode measurement of all twelve SmallLever rigs found 0 of 12 off their home stops, every one reading exactly the d the module's own note records, vent1-3 at 0.2502 against 0.250, mass1-2 at 0.2482 against 0.248, shutters at -0.4501 against -0.450 and fan1-6 at -0.4078 and -0.4094 against -0.408. Initialize no longer empties levers and lamps, so a key whose part is still parented keeps its first capture and only a key whose part has gone is re-resolved; verified by re-running, where the two throws lines are byte-identical and the captures read 54 and 54. They read 54 and 52 before, because the SAM lamps are captured by walking the keyboard and their increment sat inside the branch that added them, so the one number an operator watches for a lost capture was the one number that moved on its own. Engine was not changed and no geometry was touched: VisualFeedback gained only the capture guard and the moved increment, and workspace.MCP_LAMPTEST is runtime-only and never reaches the saved place. The probe is kept as a re-runnable instrument rather than deleted, because it is the only thing that can re-prove this matrix after any change to an address or a colour.
